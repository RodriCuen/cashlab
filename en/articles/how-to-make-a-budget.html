MONEY AND HABITS

How to make a monthly budget you can actually stick to

You don't need a complicated spreadsheet to understand where your money goes.

CASHLAB · EDUCATIONAL GUIDE · 5 MIN READ

A budget isn't about giving up the things you enjoy. It shows how much comes in, how much goes out and how much room you have for your goals.

What is a budget?

An estimate of your income and expenses over a period, usually a month. It helps you anticipate bills, spot spending that adds up and decide your priorities.

Build one in four steps

  1. Write down your net income — what you actually receive. If it varies, use a cautious estimate.
  2. List fixed expenses: rent, transport, bills, subscriptions.
  3. Estimate variable expenses: food, leisure, shopping. Check your statements instead of trusting memory.
  4. Set priorities for essentials, goals and fun.

A simple example

Say you take home $2,000 a month. If essentials total $1,200, variable spending is $500 and you set aside $200 for a goal, you have $100 of margin. These are fictional numbers; yours will differ.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting annual costs such as insurance or fees.
  • Underestimating expenses so the numbers look good.
  • Trying to change every habit at once.

Once your budget works, build an emergency fund.
