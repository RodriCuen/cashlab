What is compound interest and how does it work?

Interest that earns interest — and why time is your biggest ally.

CASHLAB · EDUCATIONAL GUIDE · 4 MIN READ

With compound interest, earnings are added to the balance and then earn returns themselves, so growth accelerates over time.

A simple example

Take a hypothetical $1,000 earning 5% a year, compounded annually. After year one you'd have $1,050; in year two the 5% applies to $1,050, giving $1,102.50. This is math, not a prediction: real products can lose money or charge fees.

Simple vs. compound

Simple interest is calculated only on the initial amount. Compound interest is calculated on the accumulated balance, so with a constant positive rate it grows faster.

What changes the result

  • Time: the longer, the stronger the effect.
  • Rate: higher rates usually come with more risk.
  • Fees and taxes: they reduce net returns.
  • Inflation: it erodes purchasing power.

It works against you, too

The same mechanism makes unpaid debts grow quickly. Check the effective rate and fees before borrowing. To start saving, read how to make a budget.
