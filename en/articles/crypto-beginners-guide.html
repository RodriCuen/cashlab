CRYPTO FOR BEGINNERS

Crypto from scratch: a beginner's guide

A plain-language explanation of what crypto is and what to check before making decisions.

CASHLAB · EDUCATIONAL GUIDE · 6 MIN READ

Crypto shows up in headlines and social feeds, sometimes as a tech revolution and sometimes as a shortcut to easy money. The reality is more nuanced: digital assets can have interesting uses, but they also carry serious risks.

What is a cryptocurrency?

A cryptocurrency is a digital asset that relies on cryptography and, usually, a distributed network instead of a single operator. Bitcoin was the first widely adopted one; thousands of projects now exist, with very different goals and risks.

What is a blockchain?

A blockchain is a shared digital ledger. Transactions are grouped into blocks that are cryptographically linked, and the network follows consensus rules to agree on what gets added. The technology does not remove risk: software bugs, fraud and lost wallet access still happen.

What to understand before you start

  1. Learn the basics: coins, tokens, wallets and exchanges are different things.
  2. Understand custody: whoever holds the keys controls the funds. Read how to store crypto safely.
  3. Check security: use unique passwords and two-factor authentication.
  4. Research the project: don't rely on influencers or ads.

Risks you shouldn't ignore

  • Volatility: prices can swing sharply in a short time.
  • Fraud: fake sites, impersonation and guaranteed-return promises are common. See scam warning signs.
  • Irreversible mistakes: a transfer to a wrong address is often unrecoverable.
  • Taxes and regulation: rules depend on your country and change over time.
